# Trinity system review — November 2026

A full review of the engine, the web app, the workspace, the Chrome extension and the Game Master, against five
goals: a voice that is quick, brief and polished; a workspace that can show and collect anything, including
credentials for a secure vault; an agent that is independent and keeps getting better; a Game Master that can
really run a table; and a complete everyday assistant. Seven reviewers read the code in parallel (about 47,000
lines); their findings were checked before anything was changed. This document says what was found, what was
changed in this pass, what was measured, and what is still open.

Status words used below: **Fixed** (changed and tested), **Built** (new in this pass, with its own tests),
**Open** (found, not changed).

| Area | What changed | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Voice | A talking brain answers in a sentence or two; the voice no longer improvises; interruptions, fillers and failures handled; pace changes without pitch | Built and tested in simulation and in a browser; **not yet heard on your pod** |
| 2. Security | Every request and socket authenticated; keys bound to their addresses; the Vite server locked down; three independent reviews | Fixed |
| 3. Vault | Encrypted logins, cards and details; a secure form on the workspace; voice commands; Settings → Vault | Built, reviewed, checked in a browser |
| 4. Extension | New pairing; sign-in and card fill from the vault; full tab and window control; stronger guards | Built; the real extension passed 93 checks in Edge; **reload it in your Chrome** |

## 1. Voice conversation

### What was wrong

The live voice is PersonaPlex, a full-duplex speech model. Until now it improvised every reply that no tool
produced, from a prompt of at most 200 characters, with no memory, no clock and no view of the board. That one
design choice explains most of what you heard:

- **Rambling and unprompted talk.** Nothing capped how long the voice spoke, noticed repetition, or kept it quiet
  when nobody had spoken. It was free to talk at mic-open, after every sentence no tool took, and again five
  seconds after every tool reply.
- **"I can't" and invented details.** Chit-chat, opinions, follow-ups ("why?") and any question the router scored
  under its threshold were answered by the small speech model. Its prompt said "Never say you can't", which invites
  false confirmations whenever no tool ran.
- **Slow knowledge answers.** Questions that did go to the text model ("ask_brain") waited for the whole answer
  before the first word, and the call didn't switch off the model's "thinking", so a thinking model could spend its
  whole token budget before saying anything.
- **Turn-handling bugs** (each confirmed in code):
  - If the routing model (Jev) failed or timed out, every fallback was silenced too, because the fallback's pick
    carried Jev's error and the firing rule skips anything with an error. Requests sat muted for eight seconds.
  - A tool that fired mid-sentence and was picked again at the end of the sentence left the voice muted for eight
    seconds.
  - Talking while "One moment." was being said cancelled the real answer behind it.
  - A second queued reply spoke right after you interrupted the first.
  - Interrupting stopped the pod but not the audio already queued in the browser, and a single word ("stop")
    never counted as an interruption.
  - Tapping the mic off right after asking dropped the answer that was on its way.
  - Typed turns that needed no tool got no answer at all during a call.
  - If the transcriber failed to connect once at warm-up it was never retried: the call ran with no tools.
  - The finished sentence was sent to Jev twice (as history and as "latest words").
  - "Clear the screen… except the timer" wiped everything, because clearing fired mid-sentence; "don't clear the
    screen" could clear it.
  - A dead link to the pod went unnoticed (silence forever).
  - Long tool output was read aloud in full (a page selection could run for ninety seconds).
- **Speed control changed the pitch.** The browser sped speech up or slowed it down by resampling, which is
  exactly what raises or lowers a voice: 0.85× is almost three semitones down. It also let playback fall further
  and further behind at slow speeds.

### What changed

- **Polished talk style (new default).** The voice no longer improvises. It is held for the whole call and speaks
  only words it is given: a tool's reply, or a reply written by a text model ("the talking brain",
  `server/src/services/talk.ts`). That model gets the persona, the last ten turns, what Trinity knows about you,
  the time, and what is on the board. Its reply is streamed a sentence at a time and capped in code: two sentences
  by default, three for a "why" or "how", six only when you ask for a story or detail. It can also answer with two
  special replies: **ACT**, when your words ask for something to be done and no tool took them (the multi-step
  solver then takes the request, instead of a refusal), and **SILENT**, for "okay", "hmm" or talk meant for someone
  else. The old behaviour is still there as the "Free" talk style in Settings → Models → How Trinity talks.
- **Started early, never late.** The talking brain starts writing the moment your sentence ends, beside Jev's last
  look at it. If a tool fires, the tool takes the turn and the draft is dropped. If the chat model has not produced a
  word in 1.2 seconds, a backup model starts beside it and the first to speak has the turn. If no model answers
  twice in a row, the voice is released to improvise for the rest of that call rather than leave you in silence.
- **Half a thought waits.** A sentence that trails off ("I want to…") gets 700 ms more before it is answered, and
  a reply that has not started is dropped when you go on talking, so one thought gets one answer.
- **Interruptions.** One clear word stops her; the browser's queued audio is flushed too; a "yeah" or "mm-hm"
  while she talks does not cut her short; talking over "One moment." only stops the filler; replies queued before
  an interruption stay silent; the mic-off tap no longer drops an answer on its way.
- **Fallbacks that work.** A fallback's pick no longer carries Jev's error; Jev is given 3.5 seconds, not 8.
- **Honest failures.** A tool that fails or asks a question is spoken in its own words; the reply writer is told
  when something did not work and can no longer "confirm" it as done.
- **Long read-outs** stop at about ninety words with "There's more. Say keep going to hear the rest."
- **An independent review of the live call** reproduced eight bugs in a lab and all are fixed: her own "Sure." or
  "Okay." heard back through the microphone could answer a waiting yes/no question (a card fill among them); the
  first tap after an idle spell could end a healthy call; a hanging model left her silent for the rest of the call; a
  late routing decision got two answers; "stop" between two sentences didn't stop her; a reply cut off by its time
  limit was spoken as half a sentence; a typed turn during the mic-off moment repeated the spoken one. A yes or a no
  now counts only when it leads what was said, and anything that spends or deletes needs a yes that says so ("yes,
  delete it").
- **Pace without pitch.** The browser now changes speed with a time stretcher: it overlaps short slices of the
  waveform instead of resampling it, so the voice keeps its pitch from 0.7× to 1.4× (measured within 0.4 %). In a
  real browser a 220 Hz test tone still reads 220.1 Hz at 0.7×, where the old method gave 154 Hz. It adds 20 to 43 ms
  of delay. At slow speeds playback no longer drifts behind the live voice: pauses are shortened to pay the time
  back, and speech itself is never dropped. Each persona can carry its own pace on top of your setting (the Game
  Master's is 0.92).
- **A default that was wrong.** A speech rate that had never been set was read as the slowest one (0.7×), so a
  fresh install spoke slow and, with the old resampling, low. It now reads as normal speed. Your own setting is
  stored as 1 and is unaffected.

### Measurements (10 November 2026, your gateway)

Time from the end of your words to the first sentence of a reply written by a text model:

| Model | First sentence | Kept the talk rules (41 test lines) |
|---|---|---|
| deepseek-v4-1-flash-uncensored (your Brain) | median 0.8 s, worst 1.7 s | 40 |
| claude-haiku-5-5 (the new backup) | about 1.0 s, steady | 40 |
| nvidia-nemotron-3-nano (the reply writer) | about 0.7 s | 29 — claims work it isn't doing |
| qwen3-next-80b | about 0.8 s | 29 |

In the simulated call (fake pod, fake transcriber, fake gateway with a 350 ms first token) the first words went
to the voice 0.6–0.9 s after the user stopped, and the improvising voice said nothing in any scenario. The same
path in a real browser, typed, showed 0.4–0.6 s to the first words.

A request that runs a tool has two small model steps besides routing: filling the tool's arguments from your
sentence, and writing the one-sentence confirmation. On your gateway (four requests, three rounds each; every model
filled every argument correctly):

| Model | Fill the arguments | Write the confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| nvidia-nemotron-3-nano (in use) | median 0.76 s, worst 1.1 s | median 0.63 s, worst 1.2 s |
| deepseek-v4-1-flash-1017-ultrafast | median 0.64 s, worst 0.83 s | median 0.66 s, worst 0.73 s |
| claude-haiku-5-5 | about 1.0 s | about 1.0 s |

So a request that needs arguments is confirmed about 1.5 to 2 seconds after you stop talking: the end-of-speech
pause (0.3 s), routing (about 0.15 s), the two steps above, and the tool itself. A tool that needs no arguments
skips the first step. A faster model changes little here; the next gain is structural (see below).

### Still open

- Nobody has listened to the result on the real pod yet.
- Speculative routing (starting Jev's final decision before the transcriber declares the sentence over) would
  save a further 100–300 ms.
- Tool turns could lose about half a second more by speaking a tool's own line where it is already a good
  sentence, instead of having a model rewrite it.
- The pod itself was not touched (it is read-only for this work).

## 2. Security

### What was wrong

The engine listened on loopback and checked only the Host and Origin headers. That is not a lock:

- **No authentication (critical).** A request with no Origin header, or from any page on any other localhost
  port, was trusted. Such a caller could read memories and the workspace, send text that the engine treated as
  your own words, plant scheduled instructions, and rewrite settings.
- **Extension pairing (critical).** The pairing token was served by the API to anyone who asked, sent in the
  socket's address, and accepted from any extension. A program that got in first could receive every browser
  command — including, later, a vault fill — or run script in your signed-in tabs.
- **Keys could be redirected (high).** Changing a service address in Settings sent the stored key to the new
  address on the next call.
- **Skill sandbox (high).** The check that keeps model-written skills away from local addresses ran inside the
  skill's own JavaScript world, where the skill could switch it off.
- **Untrusted text steering tools (high).** Page text could reach the multi-step solver, which may call any tool.
- Smaller findings: a server-side image fetcher that could be pointed at local addresses; a way for a full-page
  frame to drop its content policy; an asset fetcher that followed `..`.

### What changed

- **Every request and socket is authenticated** (`server/src/auth.ts`). A browser is linked once and gets a session
  cookie that page scripts cannot read; each request must also come from one of the app's own pages (exact origin).
  Scripts use an API key. The first browser to open the app links itself; any later one enters a six-digit code
  shown in the engine's terminal or in Settings. Forty tests cover the attack cases; the three pages were checked in
  a real browser.
- **Keys stay with their address.** A key is used only with the address it was entered for; change the address and
  the key is withheld until you enter the one for the new address. Settings updates are validated.
- **The extension's link** was rebuilt so that nothing but the paired extension can take it (section 4).
- **Skills run with no network of their own.** A skill's code runs in a separate process that cannot reach the
  network at all; when it fetches, the engine fetches for it through a checked fetcher that refuses local and private
  addresses (the same fetcher now guards pictures fetched from the web). Built-ins are frozen before a skill loads.
- **Untrusted words stay data.** Web pages, emails and search results are passed to models fenced as quoted data;
  in a multi-step job a new site, a script on a page, or anything that touches the vault, payment or sending needs the
  user's own words. Tools that use the vault, pay, sign in or send run only for the user's own words: never for the
  multi-step solver, a scheduled run, a card or a skill. This is checked where tools are offered and again where they
  run.
- **Smaller holes closed:** a full-page frame can no longer drop its content policy; the asset fetcher no longer
  follows `..`; the conversation log blanks anything shaped like a card number, key or password.
- **A second, independent review** of the login code and the settings found, and this pass fixed:
  - (critical) the Vite development server served every file in the project, the keys in `data/` included, to
    pages on other localhost ports. It now serves only the app, answers no other origin, and won't move to another
    port. (The development server you were running had this exposure until this change.)
  - a different program on the app's port (5173) would have been trusted as the app and could use your browser's
    login: the engine now accepts a browser's login only on requests that come through Trinity's own web server,
    which adds a secret kept in `data/` (which the web server no longer serves);
  - (high) a malformed Settings update could carry a saved key to a new address;
  - a free first link that came back after every browser was unlinked; settings for extra origins that let in
    sandboxed frames; a planted cookie that could lock you out; typed-address requests that could change things.

## 3. Credentials: the vault and the secure form

### What was missing

- **Nowhere to keep a secret.** Asked for a password, Trinity could only hear it. Spoken words pass through the
  transcriber, the routing model and the transcript, and could end up in memory.
- **No safe way to ask for one.** Every card on the board lives in a sandboxed frame that cannot send anything
  back, so a card could not collect input. Worse, the card tools could read password fields inside a built
  widget: "read card 3" would have read a typed password aloud.

### What was built

- **The vault** (`server/src/vault/`). It holds website logins, payment cards, your contact details and other
  secrets such as a calendar feed address. Each item is sealed on its own with AES-256-GCM. The key that seals
  them is itself locked by a key Windows keeps for your account, and optionally by a passphrase as well. The
  vault lives in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Trinity\vault`, apart from the project and its `data` folder, so it is never
  near git. A checksum over the file means the sites a login belongs to, and your spending limit, cannot be
  edited behind the vault's back.
- **The secure form.** When something secret is needed, a form opens over the workspace. It is drawn by the
  app's own page, not by a card, and the board is frozen while it is open, so nothing on the board can imitate it
  or read it. What you type goes straight to the vault. It is never put on the board, in the transcript, in a
  log, or in front of a model. A placeholder card marks the spot and holds nothing.
- **By voice.** "Save my Netflix password", "save my credit card", "save my shipping address", "what's in the
  vault" (names only), "forget my Netflix login" (she asks first). Settings → Vault lists what is saved, adds and
  removes items, sets the lock and the spending limit, and links other browsers.
- **Rules that are in code, not in a prompt.**
  - Vault, sign-in, payment and sending tools are marked sensitive. Only your own words start them: never the
    multi-step solver, a scheduled run, a card or a skill. This is enforced where tools are offered and again
    where they run.
  - A password or card you ask her to "remember" goes to the secure form, never into memory.
  - A login is only ever filled in on the exact https site it was saved for.
  - A card is filled in only after you say yes, and she never presses Pay. The security code is asked for each
    time unless you choose to keep it. Above your spending limit she asks again in the form.
  - Card tools no longer read password or one-time-code fields in built widgets.

### How it was checked

- 314 checks of its own: the encryption, the two locks, the Windows key store on this machine, the forms, the
  routes, and a canary test that plants marked secrets and then searches 127 places for them (logs, the board,
  saved state, replies, the conversation log). None were found.
- In a real browser, with made-up values: the login, card and details forms open from a typed request; Tab
  moves through the fields and stays inside the form; Escape cancels; the board is frozen while a form is open;
  card digits group as you type; a long form scrolls its fields while the buttons stay in view. After saving,
  the typed values were nowhere in the page, the board's state or the vault's own listing, which shows names,
  sites and "Visa ending 4242" only.
- An independent security review read the whole vault: no path by which a stored secret reaches a model, a log,
  the board, the transcript or an error. It found one real bug: locking the vault at the very moment its lock was
  being changed could leave it sealed under an empty key and lose every item. That, and six smaller points (the vault
  now refuses to work when the app is opened over plain http from another computer; deleting needs "yes, remove it";
  the data key changes whenever the lock does; stronger passphrase hashing; stricter site matching; stray requests
  get a fixed answer) are fixed, each with tests. The suite is now 346 checks, and the canary search covers 149
  places.

### Limits you should know

- With the default lock, any program running as your Windows user can ask Windows for the same key. That is what
  "locked by this computer" means. The passphrase lock closes that gap at the cost of typing it after idle time.
- Replacing the whole vault file with an older copy of itself is not detected.
- Chrome's own "save password?" prompt could not be observed in the test browser. The form asks browsers not to
  offer it.
- Nothing here has run on macOS or Linux.

## 4. Your browser: the extension

### What was wrong

- **Anyone could take over the link (critical).** The pairing token was handed out by the API and accepted from any
  extension, so another program could receive every browser command (see section 2).
- **Guards with gaps.** Pressing Enter, or "type … and submit", submitted a form without the check a click on a Pay or
  Send button gets. Page snapshots carried what was typed into fields, and a field with no label was named by its
  contents.
- **The wrong tab.** "Close this tab" while the app was in front ended the call. "Close the tab about cooking" closed
  the current tab. "Click the Settings tab" was read as a browser tab. A screenshot of a background tab captured the
  visible one.
- **Said but not done.** Key presses were simulated in a way pages ignore, while Trinity said "Pressed Tab". Scrolling
  moved only the window, so apps with their own scroll areas didn't move, and she still said "Scrolled down".
- **Fragile link.** Two paired browsers evicted each other every thirty seconds; an undo of a page tweak was lost when
  the extension's worker restarted; long page actions ran past the 15-second tool limit and were reported as failed.

### What changed

- **A new pairing.** The extension and the engine each make a key pair. Settings → Browser shows an eight-character
  code for ten minutes; typed into the extension's popup, it proves both sides to each other without ever being sent.
  Every later connection starts with a challenge each side must answer, so a program that answers on Trinity's port
  without the keys gets nothing. The engine accepts the socket only from the paired extension. The old token is gone.
- **Signing in and filling from the vault.** "Sign me in", "fill in my card", "fill in my address". The engine seals the
  values for that one page with a key from the handshake; the extension opens them only in the exact https page that
  was checked a moment before, fills only visible fields of a form that posts back to the same site, and then refuses
  scripts, screenshots and saved copies of that tab until it leaves the site. A card is filled only after you say yes,
  and Pay is never pressed. These tools run only on your own words.
- **Full tab and window control.** Open, close (never Trinity's own tab), switch, move, duplicate, pin, mute, group and
  ungroup, reopen what was closed, new and closed windows; "the tab about X" means that tab.
- **Guards that hold.** Enter and type-and-submit are checked like clicks; spending needs a yes that names the site and
  the total. Snapshots carry no field values. What a page says is passed to models as quoted data, and in a multi-step
  job a script or a new site needs your own words to ask for it.
- **Acting reliably.** Clicks as a real pointer sequence, keys that pages see, shadow DOM and same-origin frames, inner
  scroll areas, and honest results when something didn't happen.

### How it was checked

537 checks against a stand-in browser: the pairing and handshake (85), signing in and filling (278), and the tools
(174). Then **the real extension ran in Microsoft Edge** (it loads an unpacked extension from the command line;
Chrome no longer does) against a throwaway engine and local test pages: pairing, reconnecting after an engine restart,
every tab and window command, reading and acting on pages, and signing in and filling a card from the vault with
invented values, with every refusal holding. 93 checks passed, and five bugs found that way are fixed (the popup when
opened as a tab, a misleading message, a slow reconnect, back and forward after Trinity navigated, and "sign in" on a
page with a second form that posts elsewhere). `scripts/e2e-extension.mjs` repeats the run.

### What you need to do once

Reload the extension at `chrome://extensions` (it is version 0.2.0), then Settings → Browser → "Show a pairing code",
and type the code into the extension's popup.

### Limits

Not built: uploading a file into a page, answering a page's own pop-up dialogs, dragging. After the handshake, messages
are not signed one by one (the link is on this computer only).

## 5. The workspace

### What was wrong

- **Cards that load forever.** The engine restarts on every saved change while you work on it; a card being built
  at that moment was saved as "loading" and came back that way with nothing behind it.
- **The wrong card.** Card numbers were reused: "show the news", then "remove that", then "draw a cat", and the news
  job painted into the cat card.
- **Undo that surprised.** It brought back a whole old board: cards made since vanished, and dead loaders returned.
- **Built widgets that broke.** Forms, saved state and "#" links didn't work inside them, and their errors went
  nowhere, so a broken build just sat there.
- **Layout.** Cards could be placed off the bottom of the board, sized cards couldn't be made small, and dragging
  stalled over a widget.
- **Only five kinds of card.** Anything else (a table, a chart, a document, a file) was HTML written by a model.

### What changed

- Every card has its own identity; every job writes to its card by that identity; a build and an edit of the same
  card wait for each other.
- At start, a card left loading becomes "interrupted, say try again", and "try again" rebuilds it. Undo takes back
  one change, card by card. Limits on cards and sizes are answered with a plain sentence.
- Widgets get a proper runtime: their own content policy, working forms, saved state and "#" links. A widget that
  throws when it is first built gets one automatic repair; a build that fails is said once, briefly.
- Placement keeps cards on the board, the board reports real heights so "under" and "beside" don't overlap, and
  dragging over a widget works.
- **Five kinds drawn by the engine itself**, instantly and without a model: a sortable **table**, a **chart** (bar,
  line, area, pie, donut, scatter), a **document** (safe Markdown), **code**, and a **file** with Open and Download.
  "Put those numbers in a table", "show that as a pie chart", "show me the code for card two".
- **Cards that keep state and talk back.** A widget can keep a small state (a checklist, a choice) that survives a
  restart and shows in every window; a press in a widget becomes a line of the conversation, and may be acted on
  with board tools only, never anything sensitive, and at a limited rate.

### How it was checked

183 checks, plus 38 in a real browser (Edge): placement, drags over widgets, the widget runtime, the repair build,
the new kinds, Open and Download through the login, state shared between windows, and a real press accepted where a
scripted one is refused. A file card made by voice was opened through the login in the browser built into the Claude
app.

### Still open

The board still sends the whole board on every change rather than the card that changed; nine large cards on a full
board still overlap, because there isn't room.

## 6. The Game Master

### What was wrong

The board, saving and content generation were solid. Running a table was not:

- **Table talk became game actions.** With the router unsure, any sentence starting "I" or "we" was narrated and any
  "roll" was a roll. "Pause the game" opened a blank new game; "Thorin tries to remove the trap" removed Thorin;
  "before we begin, who wants snacks" started the game.
- **No turns.** Nothing tracked whose turn it was, initiative or rounds; the "Turn" badge followed whichever hero a
  tool touched last, and players on one microphone were told apart only by a hero's name in the sentence.
- **Dice were decoration.** A roll was never compared with a DC or an armour class; the narrator decided hits and
  damage itself, so speech could contradict the board. Monsters had no hit points or attacks.
- **Forgetful and repetitive.** The narrator's notes were cut from the end, so the newest events and dice were lost.
  "Let me see." preceded every narration, the same setup question was repeated word for word, and a clarifying answer
  ("Thorin") led nowhere.
- **Nothing left the board.** Dead monsters stayed hostile and used potions stayed in the pack.

### What changed

- **It listens properly.** Every game command waits for the end of the sentence. Starting a new game over one in
  play, removing a hero, loading a saved game and ending the campaign ask for a spoken yes first. Talk between players
  gets silence. The table gets a longer pause before a turn ends (players think aloud), the heroes' names are given to
  the transcriber, and the voice is a little slower without its pitch changing.
- **Turns and the spotlight.** Initiative, rounds, whose turn it is ("I pass", "I delay", "next"), and who is speaking.
- **A rules engine** (the free 5th-edition rules): ability checks and saves against a DC, attacks against armour class,
  damage types with resistances, conditions with their effects and durations, death saves, concentration, spell slots,
  short and long rests, and 25 spells with their rules. The dice decide; the narrator describes what the rules decided.
- **Monsters.** 74 creatures from the free rules, bounded new ones, and an encounter builder that fits the party.
- **Everything at the table.** Inventory and money (use, equip, give, drop, buy and sell), non-player characters who
  flee, die or change sides, rests, pause and resume, undo of the last twenty steps, a recap when a session ends and a
  greeting when it resumes, and answers to rules questions (52 topics) and "how much gold do I have".
- **Session zero.** The setup asks for the players, their heroes, the tone and the difficulty, once each.
- **The board** shows the turn order, hit points, conditions, and each roll with its DC and outcome.

### How it was checked

1,255 checks in five suites: the rules engine (328), turns and combat (317), a scripted multi-player session (265),
games saved before this rebuild still loading (80), and what the board is given to draw (265). The routing rules for
the table's commands were added to the router's own suite.

**Played live, with your routing model and your models** (typed through the real text path): three runs of the same
two-player evening. The first showed what the stubbed tests couldn't: "I'm playing Thorin" was taken as a player named
"Playing", "a short fantasy adventure" was ignored and "Let's begin" refused, and every question in setup got the
setup question back. The second showed another hero's move blocked by a waiting roll. All of that was fixed; in the
third run the adventure came from the first sentence, the names were right, a hero's move played while another's
check waited, "Thorin attacks the nearest enemy" with none in sight said so, table talk got silence, pause, resume and
the end of the session worked, and most turns answered in about a tenth of a second (narration about 1.5 to 2 s).

### Still open

Partial in play: cover and contested checks exist but nothing positions creatures; the bless spell is tracked but adds
no die; spells beyond the 25 only spend a slot; monsters don't cast spells or take legendary actions; loot is a simple
rule. Narration is not streamed. Not yet seen: the board in a real browser, the narration and routing with your real
models, and a game played by voice.

## 7. Memory, skills and getting better

### What was wrong

- **No history.** What was said lived only in memory and vanished on every restart; nothing could be searched later.
- **A thin picture of you.** Memories were saved line by line during the conversation, contradicting facts could sit
  side by side ("I live in Denver" next to "I moved to Austin"), and the voice and the solver never saw them.
- **Skills that never improved.** A skill was tested once, by its author; failures in use weren't counted, nothing
  repaired it, and nothing retired one that kept failing.
- **A solver that lost its way.** Its list of tools was frozen at the start, it reported finished jobs as failed when
  the last step was a tool call, and it could re-expose tools a scheduled run had hidden.
- **Reminders by voice were thin.** Fixed intervals only ("I can't skip weekends"); no list, cancel or change.

### What changed

- **A conversation log** in the engine's database with full-text search, kept 180 days; "what did we say about the
  dentist" searches it. Anything shaped like a secret is blanked before it is stored, and Settings can delete one
  conversation or all of them.
- **A profile** of who you are and the standing instructions you give ("always use Celsius"), short enough to be in
  every reply's view, editable in Settings → Memory. "I'm vegetarian" or "call me Chief" updates it.
- **Reflection instead of line-by-line saving.** At the end of a conversation, and once a night, one model call reads
  what was said and keeps what lasts: dated, compared with what is already known on that subject, duplicates merged,
  old facts fading. "Forget that I…" removes one.
- **Skills that get better.** Each skill has a version, a count of uses and failures, and a state. A failure in use
  triggers one repair, installed in place with the old version kept to roll back to; two failed repairs switch it off;
  thirty idle days archive it. A job done the same way twice becomes a skill of its own. "What skills do you have",
  "fix the currency skill", "turn it off".
- **The solver** looks again at what it can use at every step, never offers anything sensitive, can stop to ask you a
  question and carry on with the answer, and reports a finished job as finished.
- **Reminders:** weekday rules ("every weekday at eight"), "what reminders do I have", "cancel the dentist one",
  "move it to ten"; a reminder is marked done only once it has actually been said; an optional daily check-in.

### How it was checked

363 checks in five suites (memory 111, skills 63, solver 49, sandbox 52, reminders 88), and the Settings pages for
memory, skills and the check-in were driven in a real browser (19 of 19). Not yet seen with your real models: how
well reflection, the fact comparison and the skill repairs work.

### Limits

A skill's sandbox relies on Node's permission model plus frozen built-ins; there is no operating-system firewall
behind it, and a skill can still send its own arguments to a public address. The skill scanner is a first filter, not
a proof.

## 8. An everyday assistant

### Files and documents

**What was missing.** Trinity could not make or keep a file of any kind: no documents, PDFs or spreadsheets, and
nothing to open, read back or download later.

**What was built** (`server/src/services/files.ts`, `server/src/files/`, `server/src/tools/files.ts`):

- "Write a one-page summary of the plan as a PDF", "make it a Word document", "make a spreadsheet of my expenses",
  "save that note as a text file", "what files do I have", "read me the notes file", "open the expenses spreadsheet",
  "rename the report", "delete the old report" (she asks first; deleting everything needs "yes, delete all").
- Word documents with headings, lists and tables; Excel workbooks with numbers kept as numbers and dates as dates;
  CSV that a spreadsheet can't mistake for a formula; PDFs printed by the browser already on this computer (Chrome
  here), with a plain built-in writer if no browser works. No new packages.
- Files live in `data/files`, at most 10 MB each and 500 MB in all. A name you give is cleaned and never used as a path,
  and links out of the folder are refused. Long work goes on in the background and she says when it is done.
- Opening one from the app goes through the same login as everything else.

**How it was checked.** 176 checks, including the PDF printed by the real Chrome here. A Word file opened correctly
in the only Word on this computer (Word 2003 with the compatibility pack). Not yet opened: any Excel workbook (the
Excel 2003 here can't read the format), and the Word file in current Word, LibreOffice or Google Docs. In Excel 2003 a
CSV loses accents and leading zeros; current Excel reads the marker that prevents that. The keyword fallback routes 83
of 83 test phrases correctly; with your real routing model it is measured in section 9.

### Email, calendar, meetings and booking

**What was missing.** Trinity could not read an email, look at the calendar or set up a meeting.

**What was built** (`server/src/tools/assist.ts`, `server/src/services/assist/`). It works through your own browser
and the extension, on the mail and calendar pages you are already signed in to (Gmail, Outlook, Google Calendar,
Outlook calendar). No passwords or account access are handed to Trinity:

- "Check my email", "did Sam write back", "read me the second one": unread messages listed and read from the page.
- "Email Sam that I'll be ten minutes late", "reply and say thanks": a draft typed into the page's own compose form.
  It is sent only on "send it", asked first, and only while her draft is open.
- "What's on my calendar today", "am I free at three": read from the calendar page, or from a private calendar feed
  address saved in the vault.
- "Schedule a meeting with Dana on Friday at two": the event form is filled in; it is saved only on "save it", asked
  first.
- "Book the second flight": opens the booking page of a result she found. It buys nothing.

What a page says is treated as data. The email summaries are checked in code against the page (no invented
messages, no links or addresses that aren't there), the draft writer sees only your words, and nothing in a message
is ever clicked or followed.

**How it was checked.** 234 checks against recorded copies of the mail and calendar pages. **Not yet seen against
your real mailbox or calendar**: the page structures (labels, buttons, confirmation messages) are assumptions that the
first real use will confirm or correct. It works only while the extension is connected.

## 11. What you need to do once

1. **Restart nothing**: the engine and Vite pick up the changes themselves when they are copied in (one restart
   each). Your browser tab reconnects; the first browser to open the app after that is linked to the engine by
   itself. Any other browser asks for a six-digit code (shown in the engine's terminal, and in Settings → Vault →
   "Link another browser").
2. **The extension**: open `chrome://extensions`, press reload on Trinity's extension (now 0.2.1), then Settings →
   Browser → "Show a pairing code", and type the code into the extension's popup. Its old pairing does not carry over.
3. **Try a voice call** on your pod. Nobody has heard the new talk style yet; if anything about it bothers you,
   Settings → Models → "How Trinity talks" switches back to the old behaviour.
4. **Mail and calendar**: open your mail and calendar in the browser the extension is in, then ask "check my email"
   or "what's on my calendar today". These were built against recorded copies of the pages and need a first real try.
   Settings → Models → "Email & calendar" sets which sites to use.
5. Optional: Settings → Vault can switch the vault to a passphrase lock.
